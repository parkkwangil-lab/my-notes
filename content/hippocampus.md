**Anatomical Structure & Internal Circuitry**

The hippocampal formation consists of distinct subregions that form a famous unidirectional intrinsic pathway known as the **Trisynaptic Circuit**:

- **Dentate Gyrus (DG):** The primary input gateway. It receives sensory information from the cortex and performs "pattern separation," keeping similar memories distinct.
    
- **Cornu Ammonis (CA3 & CA1):**
    
    - **CA3** receives signals from DG via mossy fibers. It specializes in "pattern completion"—retrieving a full memory from a partial cue.
        
    - **CA1** acts as the main output hub of the proper hippocampus, processing CA3 signals and sending them downstream.
        
- **Subiculum:** The final relay station that routes processed information out of the hippocampus to subcortical and cortical targets.

| **Connection Pathway**      | **Associated Structures**                  | **Function**                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Entorhinal Cortex (EC)**  | Parahippocampal gyrus, Neocortex           | **Main Input/Output Highway:** The EC gathers sensory input from all neocortical areas and routes it via the _perforant path_ to the hippocampus. Processed data flows back to the neocortex for long-term storage.            |
| **Fornix**                  | Mammillary bodies, Thalamus, Septal nuclei | **Subcortical Output Tract:** A C-shaped bundle of nerve fibers. It connects the hippocampus to the mammillary bodies (forming part of the Papez circuit for memory formation) and the septal area (regulating theta rhythms). |
| **Amygdala**                | Basal & lateral nuclei                     | **Emotional Modulation:** Direct bidirectional connection. Adds emotional weight and valence (e.g., fear, reward) to memories.                                                                                                 |
| **Prefrontal Cortex (PFC)** | Prefrontal cortex (PFC)                    | **Executive Control:** Coordinates working memory, decision-making, and memory retrieval strategy.                                                                                                                             |

The entorhinal cortex serves as the ultimate anatomical gateway and information hub between the neocortex and the hippocampus, playing.a critical role in memory consolidation and spatial navigation.

Connections with the neocortex
 the neocortex(specifically high-level sensory, multimodal, and association area) does not connect directly to the hippocampus. Instead, it communicates through intermediate structures:
	 o Higher-Order Processing: Information from the visual, auditory, and somatosensory association neocortex converges into the parahippocampal gyrus before reaching the EC.
	 o Reciprocal Feedback: Once the hippocampus processes memories, output signals travel back through the deep layers of the EC and the parahippocampal gyrus to wide areas of the neocortex for long-term storage.

Connections with the Parahippocampal Gyrus
The parahippocampal gyrus is a region of cortex surrounding the hippocampus, encompassing the parahippocampal cortex (PHC), perirhinal cortex (PRC), and entorhinal cortex (EC) itself. They form a dual-stream processing pathway:
	o The "What" Stream (Perirhinal Cortex): Processes information about objects, identity, and items, feeding primarily into the lateral entorhinal  cortex.
	o The "Where/Context" Stream (Parahippocampal Cortex): Processes spatial layout, scenes, and contextual information, feeding primarily into the medial entorhinal cortex.

The Cortico-Hippocampal Circuit Loop
Information flows through this network in a well-defined loop:
	1. Neocortex --> sends high-level sensory data to the Perirhinal and Paramhippocampal Cortices.
	2. Parahippocampal Gyrus --> relays and integrates this data into superficial layers (II and III) of the Entorhinal Cortex.
	3. Entorhinal Cortex --> projects via the perforant path into the Hippocampus (Dendate Gyrus and CA3).
	4. Hippocampus --> processes the information and sends output back to deep layers (V and VI) of the Entorhinal Cortex, which then routes back through the parahippocampal gyrus to the Neocortex.

The parahippocampal gyrus links to the neocortex through several major white matter fiber tracts, with the cingulum playing a central role.

1. The Cingulum Bundle (The Limbic-Neocortical Highway)
   The cingulum is a massive, C-shaped bundle of white matter fibers that arches right above the corpus callosum.
   o The Pathway: It runs all the way from the frontal and parietal neocortex, through the cingulate cortex, and curves down into the temporal lobe to terminate directly in the parahippocampal gyrus and entorhinal cortex.
   o Its Function: This tract allows the limbic system to communicate directly with higher order association areas in the frontal and parietal lobes, integrating memory with ececutive control, attention, and emotional context. The specific section running into the temporal lobe is often called the parahippocampal cingulum.
2. The Inferior Longitudinal Fasciculus (ILF)
   Another critical tract is the ILF, which runs along the bottom of the brain.
   o The Pathway: It connects visual and associative areas of the occipial and temporal neocortex directly to the parahippocampal gyrus.
   o Its Function: This is the primary route for high-level visual data (like recognizing faces, objects, and complex scenes) to flow into the medial temporal lobe for memory encoding.
3. The Uncinate Fasciculus
   o The Pathway: This hook-shaped tract connects the anterior temporal lobe (including parts of the parahippocampal/perirhinal regions) to the frontal lobe (specifically the orbitofrontal cortex and limbic prefrontal areas).
   o Its Function: It facilitates rapid communication between emotional/memory processing in the temporal lobe and decision-making/social behavior in the frontal neocortex.
